# Flags for the client

Five things I'd resolve before this copy is final. Four are quick; the third is a real decision.

1. **Spec conflict.** The Buildings summary says 580 sf upper level; the Cottage detail says 590 sf. Same for the Cabin. Pick one — it'll end up on a spec sheet and in a lender package.

2. **Headline is still TBD.** Four options in the doc, none chosen. The comp needs one to look finished, so it ships with a recommendation, not a decision.

3. **Primary CTA is contradictory, and it matters.** The hero CTA is "Schedule a call to find out if Rhubarb is right for your backyard." But the FAQ says to use the self-serve estimate tool *before you ever talk to anyone*. Those are two different funnels: a call is high-intent and low-volume, an estimate is low-friction and captures people who aren't ready to talk to a salesperson. For a $400K considered purchase where most visitors don't yet know what an ADU costs, estimate-first will almost certainly capture more of the top of funnel and pre-qualify the calls you do take. I've written it estimate-first with the call downstream. Worth confirming with them, because it changes the shape of the page.

4. **"Get a Rhubarb. Get growing!" is two sentences for a button.** Buttons should name what happens. `Get growing` on its own is the button; the full line works as a closing headline, which is where I've put it.

5. **Three unresolved placeholders** in their doc that the site will need: total timeline design→move-in, Denver/Colorado property tax assessment detail, and ROI or payback figures. The timeline one is the most conspicuous absence on a page selling schedule certainty.

**Dropped from the site copy:** the open house. A specific Saturday at a specific address is a campaign asset, not evergreen homepage furniture — it belongs in a banner or a Klaviyo send, not baked into the layout.

**On the courtyard:** it stays, but as texture inside `what-it-is`, not as the page's thesis. It's a nice observation about how the lot gets used, and it isn't the reason anyone buys a $400K building.
